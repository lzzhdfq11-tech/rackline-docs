B2B pricing, quantity rules and price breaks

Rackline displays Shopify’s native B2B features: company context, catalog prices, quantity rules and volume pricing. You set these up in your Shopify admin and the theme shows and enforces them on product pages, lists, quick order and the cart. No B2B app is needed.

Company context

When a customer who buys for a company signs in, the header shows a company chip with the company name and the current location. The chip links to the customer account, where buyers who order for several locations switch location. The mobile menu drawer shows the same information as a card with a “Change location” link.

The Trade welcome section adapts to who is viewing:

  • Guests see your heading, text, benefit points and an “Apply for a trade account” button.
  • B2B buyers see a greeting, their company and location, and shortcuts to quick order, orders and reorders, the cart summary and the catalog.
  • Signed-in customers without company access see their own heading and text.

In the theme editor, the “Preview in the theme editor” setting switches between the guest and trade customer versions; customers always see the version that matches their account.

Company prices

All prices come from Shopify. When a B2B buyer is signed in, Shopify returns the price from the catalog of their current location, and Rackline shows it on product pages, collection tables and grids, product lists, search suggestions, the quick order page and the cart. The theme never calculates prices or shows a price that Shopify will not charge.

Quantity rules

Quantity rules set a minimum, a maximum and an increment for a variant. Rackline reads the rule for each variant and applies it to every quantity field: product pages, table rows, the variant order table, quick order lines and the cart.

  • Visible hint. When Show quantity rules next to quantity fields is on and a rule is not the default, the field shows a hint such as Min 10 · Max 500 · Increments of 5.
  • Snapping. A typed quantity that breaks the rule is changed to the nearest valid value and the reason is shown. For example, with increments of 5, typing 17 becomes 15 with the message “Order in multiples of 5”.
  • Plus and minus buttons step by the increment and stop at the minimum and maximum.
  • Items already in the cart count toward the maximum; the field shows how many are in the cart, or that the maximum is already in the cart.

Volume pricing as a price ladder

Volume pricing (quantity price breaks) lowers the unit price at set quantities. Rackline shows it as a price ladder: a table of tiers, starting with the base price, with the unit price and the saving for each tier.

  1. 1+$5.00Base price
  2. 10+$4.75Save 5%
  3. 50+$4.50Save 10% · current
  4. 100+$4.25Save 15%

Example values. In the Rackline preset the tiers are drawn as rising steps; Ledger draws a ruled tier table and Crate a case grid of tiers.

  • The tier that matches the quantity in the quantity field is highlighted and updates as the buyer changes the quantity.
  • A live line shows “Your unit price” and “Subtotal for” the chosen quantity.
  • The ladder appears only for variants that have price breaks.
  • In the Price block you can turn the ladder off or show it in its compact form.
  • With Theme settings → Product lists → Show volume pricing in lists, collection tables, product lists and product cards show a compact ladder such as 10+ $4.75 · 50+ $4.50.

Price visibility

The setting Show prices to (Theme settings → Pricing and trade) has two options:

  • Everyone (default): all visitors see prices and can buy.
  • Signed-in customers only: guests see a “Sign in to see prices” link instead of prices. Quantity fields, add to cart buttons and accelerated checkout buttons are hidden for guests, the quick order page asks guests to sign in, and product structured data leaves out prices.

Tax note

Tax note next to prices adds the text “Tax included” or “Excl. tax” after prices, or nothing. It is text only: Rackline does not calculate tax. Shopify calculates taxes at checkout, and the cart summary states whether taxes are included.

Trade account application

The Trade application template is a form for businesses that want a trade account. It asks for the company name, contact name, email, phone, business registration or tax ID, website, type of business, expected monthly order volume and a message, with an optional consent checkbox. The form is sent through Shopify’s contact form to your store email, tagged with your email marker so you can filter these emails. You then create the company in your admin. See Getting started.
