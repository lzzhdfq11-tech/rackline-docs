Getting started with Rackline

This guide takes you from installing the Rackline Shopify theme to a published B2B storefront. B2B prices, quantity rules and volume pricing are set up in your Shopify admin; the theme then shows them on every page. Plan about an hour, plus the time to set up your B2B companies and catalogs.

1. Install the theme

  1. Open the Rackline listing on the Shopify Theme Store while you are logged in to your store.
  2. Choose a preset (Rackline, Ledger or Crate) and select Try theme. Shopify adds an unpublished copy to Online Store → Themes.
  3. Select Customize to open the theme editor. Your live store does not change until you publish.
  4. When you are ready, buy the theme from the Shopify Theme Store and publish it.
Moving from another theme?

Products, collections, pages, menus, metafields and B2B data stay in your store. Only theme settings and section content are set up again.

The preset only sets starting values. You can change the visual style in Theme settings → Visual style, and fonts, colors and density in their own settings groups. See Presets.

3. Set up Shopify B2B in your admin

Rackline does not store prices or rules itself. It reads them from Shopify’s native B2B features, which you set up in your Shopify admin:

  • Companies and locations. Create a company for each business customer, add its locations and the customers who buy for it (Customers → Companies).
  • Catalogs. Assign a B2B catalog to each company location to set which products the location can buy and at what prices.
  • Quantity rules and volume pricing. In a B2B catalog, set minimum, maximum and increment quantities and quantity price breaks for products or variants.
  • Payment terms and checkout options are also part of Shopify B2B; the theme mentions payment terms in the cart for B2B buyers and Shopify applies them at checkout.
Plan and accounts

Shopify B2B features are available on Shopify plans that include B2B (for example Shopify Plus). Check Shopify’s current plan comparison for your store. Shopify B2B also requires customer accounts (the newer customer accounts, not legacy accounts).

Once a B2B customer signs in, the theme shows their company and location, their catalog prices, quantity rules and price ladders. Read B2B pricing for what appears where.

4. Create the Quick order page

  1. In your admin go to Online Store → Pages and select Add page. Name it, for example, “Quick order”.
  2. Under Theme template, choose order-pad and save.
  3. In the theme editor open Theme settings → Pricing and trade and select the page as Quick order page.

The header then shows a Quick order button (Header section setting “Show quick order button”), the mobile menu links to it, and the Trade welcome section offers it as a shortcut. Details: Quick order.

5. Create the Trade application page

  1. Add another page, for example “Apply for a trade account”, and choose the theme template apply.
  2. In Theme settings → Pricing and trade, select it as Trade account application page.
  3. Open the page in the theme editor to set the business type options, monthly volume options, consent checkbox label and the email marker.

Applications arrive in your store email through Shopify’s contact form. You review them and create the company in your admin; the theme does not create accounts automatically. The utility bar can show an “Apply for a trade account” link, and the Trade welcome section shows an apply button to guests.

Optional: create a Brands page with the template brands. It shows featured brand cards and an A–Z list of every product vendor in your store.

6. Add aisle (zone) codes

Aisle codes are the short codes such as A1 or B2 that Rackline prints on navigation items, collection lists and product labels. They are decorative: the collection name always carries the meaning, and screen readers skip the codes.

Metafields the theme reads
OwnerNamespace and keyTypeUsed for
Collectioncustom.zone_codeSingle line text, e.g. B2Aisle code on menus, collection lists and products in the collection
Collectioncustom.zone_colorColor (optional)Custom zone color; text color is chosen for contrast
Productcustom.bin_locationSingle line text, e.g. A-12-03 (optional)Printed on the product label strip instead of the SKU
Productcustom.documentsList of files (optional)Files in the Downloads block (the key is set in the block)

Create the definitions in Settings → Custom data. Without a custom.zone_code, lists and menus get position codes (A1, A2, B1 …) in the order they appear. A code without a color takes its color from its first letter.

7. Set up menus

  • Main menu (Header section): up to three levels. Top-level items with children open a full-width panel; second-level links become column headings with third-level links below. On desktop, top-level items that link to collections show an aisle letter from the collection’s zone code, or the next letter in menu order.
  • Customer account menu (Header section): links shown with the account button.
  • Utility bar link menu: small links above the header, such as Help or Contact. On phones they can move into the menu drawer (“Secondary menu in mobile drawer”).
  • Footer menus: add Menu blocks to the footer.

8. Review the key settings

  • Pricing and trade: Show prices to everyone or signed-in customers only; tax note; quantity rule hints; trade pages; quote requests.
  • Stock: hidden, in stock / low stock, or exact quantity, and the low stock threshold.
  • Cart: side panel or cart page, order notes, purchase order number (optional or required), CSV download and accelerated checkout buttons.
  • Logo and business: logo, favicon and the legal business name, sales phone and email used in structured data.
  • Product lists: default collection view (table or grid) and whether lists show volume pricing.

Filters on collection and search pages come from Shopify’s Search & Discovery app, where you choose which filters appear. Complementary products on product pages are also set up in that app. See Settings for every option.

9. Check and publish

  • Preview the store as a guest and as a signed-in B2B customer to compare prices and the company chip.
  • Add a product with quantity rules and price breaks to the cart and check the price ladder and quantity hints.
  • Paste a few SKUs into the quick order page, including one that does not exist.
  • Place a test order with a PO number and check that it appears on the order.
  • Check the home page, a collection, a product and the cart on a phone.
  • Publish the theme in Online Store → Themes.
